# The Adult Repertoire Ladder: From Foundations to Passacaglia
*成人经典进阶曲目阶梯：从双手协调到《阿甘正传》《成都》与《帕萨卡利亚》*

---

## Pedagogical Philosophy of Adult Repertoire

Children are frequently subjected to years of nursery rhymes, marching tunes, and unmusical mechanical drills. For an adult, this approach is emotionally deadening.

Adults possess deep musical discernment. You should play pieces that are **harmonically rich and aesthetically rewarding** from your very first months at the keyboard. 

This ladder outlines five progressive milestones, analyzing the specific technical hurdles and interpretive nuances of works you can realistically conquer through self-directed study.

---

## Level 1: Grounding & Landmark Literacy

### 1. Minuet in G Major (BWV Anh. 114) — Christian Petzold / J.S. Bach Notebook
- **Historical Context:** Preserved in the 1725 Notebook for Anna Magdalena Bach; originally attributed to J.S. Bach, composed by Christian Petzold.
- **Technical Milestone:** Establishing clean two-hand independence in 3/4 time.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - The left hand plays steady quarter notes. Treat these as a walking bass line: keep them detached, buoyant, and dynamic level *piano* ($p$).
  - The right hand must articulate the two-note slurs (down-up wrist motion) on measures 1–4.
  - Avoid rushing the sixteenth-note turns.

### 2. Ode to Joy (Chorale Theme) — L. van Beethoven
- **Technical Milestone:** Seamless transitions between I, IV, and V7 chords.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - Keep the left hand close to the keys when switching from C major ($I$) to G7 ($V^7$). Glide rather than lift your palm.

## Level 2: Polyphony & Expressive Voicing

### 1. Gymnopédie No. 1 — Erik Satie
- **Technical Milestone:** Wide left-hand leaps and subtle half-pedaling.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - Left-hand jump: Play the low bass note (root), float your wrist upward in a graceful arc, and glide across to the mid-register chord cushion.
  - The melody must sing like an ancient Greek flute. Keep it *cantabile* while the accompaniment whispers.
  - Change pedal immediately *after* striking the low bass note (syncopated pedaling) to catch the resonance without a break.

### 2. Arabesque Op. 100 No. 2 — Friedrich Burgmüller
- **Technical Milestone:** Rapid 5-finger fluidity and snap-release articulation.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - Practice the fast A-minor running passages in **dotted rhythms** (long-short, short-long) to eliminate finger dragging.
  - Let forearm rotation drive the sixteenth notes, not isolated finger wiggling.

## Level 3: Contemporary Lyricism & Cinematic Drama

### 1. Chengdu (成都) — Piano Solo Arrangement
- **Technical Milestone:** Wide left-hand arpeggios (10th spans) and vocal rubato.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - The left-hand accompaniment often spans octaves and tenths. If your hands cannot reach a tenth comfortably, roll the chord lightly from bottom to top with a supple wrist motion.
  - Breathe naturally between phrases as if you were singing the lyrics aloud.

### 2. Game of Thrones (Main Theme) — Ramin Djawadi
- **Technical Milestone:** Relentless 6/8 syncopated ostinato and dynamic escalation.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - The left-hand cello-like motif ($C-D-Elat-F$) must maintain a strict, metronomic drive. Practice with the metronome set to eighth notes ($d. = 58-66$).
  - Build the crescendo over several minutes from a quiet whisper ($p$) to a roaring battle anthem ($fff$).

### 3. Prelude in E Minor Op. 28 No. 4 — Frédéric Chopin
- **Technical Milestone:** Microscopic chromatic inner voice leading and extreme emotional restraint.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - The left hand plays repeating eighth-note chords that sink downward by chromatic half-steps. Play these *pianissimo* ($pp$), listening intently to each subtle harmonic color shift.
  - The right-hand melody consists of long, suspended sighs. Release arm weight into the repeated B's without harshness.

## Level 4: Fluidity, Wide Arpeggios & Pure Touch

### 1. Forrest Gump Suite (Feather Theme) — Alan Silvestri
- **Technical Milestone:** Delicate high-register cascading triplets and floating arpeggio releases.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - The high piano notes represent the falling feather. If struck with a stiff finger, they sound piercing and harsh. Drop the arm weight from above with soft, cushioned fingertips.
  - Maintain a steady left-hand bass foundation so the right hand can weave freely across octaves.

### 2. Nuvole Bianche — Ludovico Einaudi
- **Technical Milestone:** Rhythmic endurance, hypnotic harmonic ostinato, and two-against-three layers.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - Prevent forearm burning by rotating your wrist in continuous micro-ovals during repetitive 4-chord cycles. Never freeze your wrist.
  - **Recommended Mentor Companion:** Follow [Michael Maiber's YouTube Tutorials](https://www.youtube.com/@MichaelMaiber). The author learned virtually all of his Ludovico Einaudi repertoire through Michael's phrase-by-phrase slow breakdowns alongside persistent personal struggle. Pair Michael's visual fingering cues with the official sheet music to overcome syncopation and cross-hand transitions.

## Level 5: Grand Architecture & Virtuosity

### 1. Passacaglia (Theme & Variations) — G.F. Handel / arr. Johan Halvorsen
- **Technical Milestone:** Grand baroque Chaconne architecture, high-speed sixteenth-note voice alternation, and dramatic orchestral texture.
- **Harmonic Blueprint:** The monumental 8-measure progression:
  $$G	ext{ minor} ightarrow C	ext{ minor} ightarrow F ightarrow Blat ightarrow Elat ightarrow C	ext{ minor} ightarrow D ightarrow G	ext{ minor}$$
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - **Do not rush the theme:** The opening chords must stand like classical Greek marble columns. Drop deep arm weight into the chords and lift with a dignified breath.
  - **The running variations:** Break the rapid sixteenth-note patterns into 1-measure loops. Practice with hands separate, then slowly stitch them together.
  - **Memory Anchor:** Memorize the bass harmonic progression. Even when your hands are flying across registers, your mind remains anchored to the tonic key center.

### 2. Nocturne in E-flat Major Op. 9 No. 2 — Frédéric Chopin
- **Technical Milestone:** Classic 12/8 waltz bass line and unmeasured bel-canto coloratura runs.
- **Practice Strategy:**
  - The left hand plays: low bass note (beat 1) followed by two buoyant middle-register chords (beats 2 and 3). Never let the middle chords become heavy.
  - Right-hand fiorituras (11-note, 22-note runs): Do not try to mathematically divide them. Let the left hand keep strict time while the right hand floats across the keys like liquid silk.
